Keeping a toddler happily busy without a screen doesn't need to be
complicated — or expensive. Here are five of my favorite low-prep activities,
each one powered by nothing more than a home printer, scissors, and maybe a
glue stick.

## 1. The quiet-time busy book

Print a [busy book](/shop/), cut out the pieces, and let your little one
match, sort, and stick. Laminating the pages (or slipping them into plastic
pockets) with a bit of velcro turns it into a reusable quiet book that
survives hundreds of play sessions. Perfect for restaurants, flights, and
waiting rooms.

## 2. Shadow matching with flashcards

Lay out a few animal flashcards and their "shadows," and ask your toddler to
reunite each animal with its silhouette. It looks like a game, but it's
building visual discrimination — a real pre-reading skill.

## 3. Rainbow letter tracing

Instead of tracing a letter once with a pencil, trace it five times with five
different crayons. The repetition builds muscle memory, and the rainbow
effect feels like art, not practice. Our
[alphabet tracing worksheets](/shop/) are designed with extra-large letters
for exactly this.

## 4. Color hunt coloring pages

Print a coloring page, then turn it into a scavenger hunt: "Can you find
something *yellow* in the picture? Color it!" You're sneaking in color
recognition and vocabulary while they think they're just coloring a duck in
a bonnet.

## 5. Count-and-cover with dry beans

Print a counting worksheet and hand over a bowl of dried beans or buttons.
Your toddler covers each object as they count it out loud. Tactile, quiet,
and endlessly repeatable.
